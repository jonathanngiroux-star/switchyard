# Replacement cost sheet

The one-page answer to "why switch." Compare your current line item to
Switchyard self-hosted, then run the dry-run to see exactly what survives
the migration. Incumbent numbers are list-price ranges seen in mid-market
contracts — verify your own invoice.

**Note: there is no hosted Switchyard tier today.** All comparisons below
are *incumbent vs. self-hosted Switchyard* (free, MIT). Hosted tiers are
planned (`docs/pricing.md`) but not yet purchasable.

## Scenario A: LaunchDarkly at a 50–100 person company

| Line item | LaunchDarkly | Switchyard self-hosted | Annual delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feature flags, 50 seats, SSO | $12,000–$50,000/yr (contract-dependent; SSO gated to upper tiers) | **$0** — one binary + SQLite | **save $12k–$50k** |
| Migration consulting (to leave LD) | 2 engineer-weeks ≈ $8,000 one-time | `switchyard migrate --from=launchdarkly --dry-run` — free, minutes, with a fidelity report before you commit | **save ~$8k** |
| Experimentation add-on | often bundled, often unused | not built (scope lock) — you keep your LD contract if you need it | neutral |

**Migration risk:** `migrate --from=launchdarkly --dry-run --format=json`
prints the exact diff and every unmappable construct *before* anything
changes. Fidelity target ≥90% on rules+segments; the gap list is the
negotiation document for what stays on LD.

**The trade you make:** Switchyard self-hosted has no vendor SLA — you
hold the pager. If that trade stops making sense at your company size,
that is exactly when a future hosted tier becomes relevant.

## Scenario B: Unleash self-hosted on Kubernetes

| Line item | Unleash-on-K8s | Switchyard self-hosted | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure | cluster namespace, Postgres, Redis, ingress | one binary + one SQLite file | **less infra to run** |
| Operations labor | upgrades (breaking changes are frequent), DB migrations, Redis care — ~2 engineer-days/month ≈ **$12k–$24k/yr** fully loaded | `switchyard serve`; upgrades are one binary swap, SQLite migrates on open (proven by tests) | **save the labor** |
| Migration | re-define flags by hand | `switchyard migrate --from=unleash --dry-run` — strategies, constraints, variants, segments mapped; 10 named gap types | **days → minutes** |
| Price | "free" (you pay in engineer time) | free | — |

**The honest framing:** Unleash is free software; Unleash-on-Kubernetes
is not free. Switchyard's pitch to an Unleash operator is not features —
it is the ops budget.

## Scenario C: Greenfield (no incumbent)

Don't pay LD pricing to start. Self-host Switchyard free, generate SDKs in
three languages from one snapshot. If a hosted Switchyard ever ships, you
can move to it for backups/upgrades/SSO instead of doing them yourself.

## What you give up switching to Switchyard today

Stated plainly, because procurement will find it anyway:

- **Experimentation / Bayesian stats** — not built (scope lock until 10
  invoiced teams). LD's experimentation users should keep LD.
- **LD big-segment membership** (unbounded segments) — imported as gaps,
  named in the fidelity report.
- **Streaming SDK updates** — v0.1 SDKs are snapshot-based; regenerate on
  deploy. Daemon/polling refresh is on the roadmap, not shipped.
- **Weighted multi-variant rollouts** — map to the dominant variant, gap
  reported.
- **A vendor to call** — self-hosted means you hold the pager. That is the
  deal today.

The fidelity reports (`docs/fidelity/launchdarkly.md`,
`docs/fidelity/unleash.md`) are the authoritative gap lists, generated by
the CLI and CI-checked — not marketing copy.
